# LDraw render styles (remastered layer)

How LEGO and LDraw models are drawn in genuinely different styles, and which of those fit the remastered `?hd` layer: real-time WebGL 2 with three.js, on the phone pixel budget of PR #122 (no extra full-screen pass, no extra render target, a few texture taps per fragment at most). Classic is never touched; the styles change only the LDraw plastic and its edge lines (`ldraw-look.ts` (`packages/ldraw`, module `material`), `ldraw-look-shader.ts`, `ldraw-plastic.ts`).

The earlier presets (Manual, Studio, Photo, LEGO Movie) all moved along one axis, gloss: more clear coat, sharper reflections, then wear on top. The styles below each change a different axis: the shading model, the edge treatment, the colour handling or the output.

## What was built

Every look keeps `MeshPhysicalMaterial` as the material class (the drivers' skin colours and the pickup glow find the plastic by that class) and keeps the emissive term (the pickup bricks' glow, the faces' room light). The stylised looks replace only the lighting at the end of the fragment shader (`opaque_fragment`): they read three.js's directional-light uniforms, use the studio's directions and balance (not its strength, so a figure, a car and a menu look alike), and encode the result to sRGB once, so the library's colours reach the screen as LDraw names them (the renderer has no colour management; the flesh parts are not restored for these looks, `getIsLookEncoded`). Their defines are written into the shader source, not the material, because a drawing's copy of a material resets its defines. Every look still casts real shadows (the shadow pass draws the casters with its own depth material). No look adds a pass, a render target or a draw call.

- **Manual** (default, unchanged): glossy plastic with a clear coat, a small studio cube map, dimmed edge lines.
- **Photo** (the user's favourite, the quality bar): unchanged, except that rubber is matte and the catch-lights skip rubber and the conditional lines (the white tyres and spokes).
- **Instructions**: the modern LEGO building-instructions page. Colour almost flat (a soft half-Lambert gradient from 0.72 to 1.08 of the colour), no highlight, no reflections, the LDraw lines in the library's edge colour (a black part gets the grey edge, as in the booklets).
- **Toon**: three hard bands (0.5, 0.75, 1) of the studio's light, anti-aliased with `fwidth`, a hard white highlight, black ink from the LDraw lines and conditional lines (the silhouettes of studs, tyres and heads).
- **Game**: TT Games-like. Colours saturated 1.3 times, a wrapped ramp so the shadow side never goes black, a broad soft highlight, baked occlusion at 0.6 and a thin Fresnel rim; no lines.
- **Retro 1999**: the 1999 look, so the LDraw cars sit in the Classic track. Faceted normals (`flatShading`), Lambert plus ambient, no highlight, no lines, and the output quantised to 16-bit colour (RGB 565) with a 4 x 4 ordered dither.
- **Material categories** in every look (`ldraw-material-category.ts`): rubber (tyres, `Rubber_Black`), transparent, chrome, metallic and plastic each get their own surface; see [remastered-ldraw-cars.md](remastered-ldraw-cars.md).

Tried and dropped: **Clay** (matte, colours washed toward clay, full occlusion): in a race the cars became grey blobs that could not be told apart, and the minifigure decals (the original's pictures) stayed coloured and glossy beside the clay. **Studio** and the **LEGO Movie** look were withdrawn (only more gloss; wear that read badly). A realistic "toy photo" beyond Photo was not built: what would make it different (path-traced bevel highlights, subsurface, soft shadows between parts) does not fit a phone.

**Verification.** Headless Chrome on the dev server (port 5287), `?hd&look=<style>` for each look: main menu, builder, build-a-driver, racer select and a race start on Imperial Grand Prix; the sheet is `~/ldraw-gallery/styles/styles-comparison.png`, the wheels before and after `wheels-photo-before-after.png`, plain studs `stud-logos-on-off.png`. Race frame time on the development Mac's GPU (1280 x 720, median of 120 frames): 2 to 4 ms in every look, within the run-to-run noise.
