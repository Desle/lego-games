# LDraw wheels and drivers (remastered layer)

With `?hd` in development and Bricks = LDraw (the default), every racer's wheels and driver (and a car with a complete model) are drawn from the official LDraw parts library, in the same plastic as the [LDraw cars](remastered-ldraw-cars.md): the player, the computer opponents and the champions, in the race, the Racer Select showcase, the driver editor, the main menu and the race cinemas. Physics, the simulation and the original's skeleton animations are untouched: the LDraw parts follow the poses the viewer already computes. Read [remastered.md](remastered.md) first for the rule. Code: `packages/lego-racers-web/src/modules/remastered/services/ldraw-racer/`.

## The neutral hooks (Classic side)

Classic code knows nothing about LDraw. It has two extension points (`src/shared/viewer-extensions/`) and one helper (`track-viewer/services/racer-drawing`):

- `getCarDrawing(request)`: a complete car (body, chassis, wheels). The request is the archive, the champion's body code (such as `RRCM`) or the built design (which names its chassis), and the chassis's wheel model. The answer (`CarDrawing`) has an object, `applyPose({ body, wheels, opacity })` and `dispose`. A pose part is what Classic already computes: the group matrix (frame, position, lean, part scale), whether it is drawn, and the animation state of the wheels (spin and steering).
- `getDriverDrawing(request)`: a complete driver, from the five part bytes or the driver model, with `applyPose({ matrix, isVisible, animationState, opacity })` (the skeleton pose) and `animate`.
- Either answers `null` for the original, and then the whole piece is the original's, never mixed. `attachCarDrawing` and `attachDriverDrawing` serve the race and the ghosts (parts the viewer moves every frame: the drawing follows their groups); `showCarDrawing` and `showDriverDrawing` serve the places that move a model's group as a whole: the builder (with its wheels), the showcase turntable, the main menu figure, the showcase driver, the driver editor and the cinemas.
- The ghost of the player's best run is drawn from the same drawings at the alpha of the ghost; the champion's ghost (GHOST.WDB models) stays the original's.

## Sources of a complete car (`ldraw-racer-model/`)

1. `~/ldraw-models/<CODE>.ldr|.mpd` (a hand-built champion car; `LEGO_LDRAW_MODELS_ROOT` moves the folder), served by the dev route `/__development/ldraw-models/` with an `index.json` read before the first frame. The file is in the car's frame, written in LDraw axes (see the folder's README, generated from `reference-kit/ldraw-models-readme.md`).
2. The LDraw Official Model Repository: no set of its 1,470 is a LEGO Racers car (checked by name and year, 2026-10), so there is no set-to-car table; a downloaded model can be saved as `<CODE>.mpd`.
3. A garage car: `createLDrawCarDocument` writes its brick list as the same kind of document, with its chassis part as a reference to `chassis/<CHASSIS>.ldr` (recipe frame: the chassis brick's footprint corner), or, with `?hd&chassis=game`, a converted unofficial part (`ldraw-chassis/chassis-fallback.ts`, studs on every flat cell).

Wheels are added when the file has none (the library sets below, on the original's wheel bones). If it has some (parts of the library's category `Wheel` or `Tyre`), they are found, taken out of the body and each follows the original wheel nearest to it. A minifigure in a file is removed: the driver is its own drawing. The dev panel lists which of the 20 cars and 12 chassis have a model.

## Reference kit

`pnpm --filter @lego-games/lego-racers-web run reference-kit` writes `~/ldraw-models/reference/<CODE>.svg|.txt` for the 20 champions and `reference/chassis/<CHASSIS>.svg|.txt` for the 12 chassis (four orthographic views over a stud grid, dimensions, LDraw colour names, wheel mounts), `~/ldraw-models/README.md`, `_convention-test.ldr` and `chassis/_fallback/*.dat`. A spec (`convention-test-model.spec.ts`) proves the axis conventions.

## Wheels

`wheel-analysis` reads the original's wheel model (`<chassis>JMW`, three bones: the model frame and the two axles): its triangles are welded by position into pieces, a hubcap beside a tyre is one wheel, and each wheel gets its bone, its centre in the bone's frame, its axle (the axis whose two other extents match), radius and width. All 24 chassis have four wheels, 40 to 70 LDraw units across (a stud is 64 raw units and 20 LDraw units, the same scale as the bricks).

`wheel-kind` says what kind of wheel a chassis model has, from the material of its wheel model (read from the models and their pictures): the wooden wagon wheel (`wgnwhl`, `dachas0`), the small hex slick with a four-spoke hub (`mate2144`), the knobbly tyre (`mate2628`, `bb_tire`), the knobbly tyre on a twelve-spoke hub (`advn_tr1`, `bvb_1647`) and the ribbed drums that two wheels fuse into (`ad_car`, `gmtires`). `ldraw-wheel-sets` lists, for each kind, the library parts of that kind with their measured size: a wagon wheel is the one wooden part `2470` (`4489a`, `33211`, `33212` for bigger ones) in reddish brown, without a tyre; a slick is the smooth tyre `30028b` on `30027b`; a knobbly tyre and a drum are the tread tyre and rim pairs (`51011` + `4624`, `56890` + `6014b`, `30391` + `11208`, `2696` + `2994`, `2857`/`4267` + `2695`, `4410` + `6595c02`); a spoked knobbly wheel is `2346` on the twelve-spoke rim `30155`. The set of the wheel's kind nearest in diameter is scaled to the wheel's exact size; a drum at least 1.5 times as wide as the tyre (`dcchas0`, `gm_chas0`) is one tyre stretched along its axle to the drum's width (two tyres side by side read as eight wheels on the car). The rim takes the colour the original paints its hub: the most common bright colour of the wheel's picture under the flat faces of its end caps (a car's picture is shared with its body, so the picture as a whole says nothing); a wagon wheel is always reddish brown. Each wheel is placed with the bone's matrix of the pose, so the animation spins and steers it. **Hypothesis**: the material-to-kind table was made by looking at all 24 game wheels beside the parts (`~/ldraw-gallery/wheels/`), not read from the game; the blue drum of `gm_chas0` still has black tyres.

## Drivers

A driver is five part bytes (hat, face, torso, legs, expression). The race's computer drivers are fixed models (`rrpelvis`), but the driver database holds their five bytes (the port's `unreadBytes`: `RR` is `1 28 15 18 0`: `helmtrr`, `rr_`, `rr_chst`, `rr_leg`), so a champion is built the same way as a player's driver.

- **Parts** (`minifig-assembly`): hips `3815b`, legs `3816b` and `3817b` (a peg leg `2532` for the legs of type 1, `leg_peg`), torso `973`, head `3626b`, arms `3818` and `3819`, hands `3820` (a hook `2531` for the left hand of a torso of type 1, `vb_chst` and `cr_chst`), and the hat. The figure is assembled in LDraw's frame as the library's complete figures stack the parts (`minifig-geometry`; `15396c02`, `4480c05`, `980`, checked against the parts' own geometry): from the top of the hips (`3815`'s origin), the torso 32 above (its hem on the hips), the head and its hats 24 above the torso (its rim on the shoulders; `979`/`980` put it 28 above, off the shoulders), the legs 12 below on the hip axle, the arms at (±15.552, 9) of the torso turned 9.78 degrees onto its slanted sides and the hands where `973c01` holds them. It is scaled 3.2 raw units to an LDraw unit, so a minifigure is as big as a brick of the car. The hips' height in the model is measured, not guessed: the menu figure's hip block tops out at z 32.8 and its feet at -95.2, exactly 40 LDraw units (the hips' top to the soles) apart, and its torso then ends at 135.2 against the original's 135.3; a seated driver's legs lie on the bottom of the race driver's pelvis block (-0.9), which puts the hips' top at 69.5 (the original's shoulders are 14 raw units lower: its seated figure is squatter, and sinking the legs into the seat would be worse).
- **Real parts by catalogue entry** (`ldraw-minifig-parts`): every hat, face, torso and pair of legs of `BODYPART.PCB` has a row of data naming the library part and colour that stands for it, with a confidence and a source (the official figure of the character, from Rebrickable's CSV downloads joined to LDraw through the part headers' `Rebrickable` keywords, or a print of the same theme and era). The review table, to correct, is [remastered-minifig-parts.md](remastered-minifig-parts.md). A champion's parts are his catalogue entries (`RR` is `1 28 15 18 0`), so he gets the printed head, torso, legs and hats of his official figure (`JT`: `3626bpa3`, `973pa3`, `30167`). **Hypothesis**: the character names (`kk` King Kahuka, `cr` Captain Redbeard, ...) are read off the part prefixes and pictures, not from the game.
- **Colours and pictures** (`minifig-surfaces`): read from the figure builder's own model of the bytes, so they are the original's. They are used only where no library part is matched: the plain head with the face picture as a decal (`ICB_CHAR.GCB`'s face card shows u 0.27 to 0.72 and v 0.375 to 0.688 of the 64 x 64 picture; v counts down the bitmap's rows), and the plain torso (`973`) whose chest picture is a decal clipped to the torso's front face (28.7 wide at the shoulders, 38 at the hem), so it no longer reaches the sides or arms. Heads and hands (`isSkin` in the part plan) get their colours back as the library names them and a 30 % share of them with no lamp on it (`skin-colors`): the viewer outputs linear light with no encoding, so a lit yellow came out orange and dark. With the texture pack on (`?hd`) the pictures come from the pack as they load.
- **Skeletons** (`minifig-rig`): the race driver's seated skeleton (`pelvis`, `torso`, `arm_l`, `arm_r`, `dummy01`, `head`; bind pose: legs forward, arms reaching 14 degrees below the horizontal, measured as the principal axis of their vertices) and the menus' standing one (`rr`, `hr`, `rp`, `hp`: 29 bones, bind pose arms out sideways). The rig names the bone that carries the figure (the pelvis), the one the torso turns with, the head's, and for each leg and arm which way it points: a bone's turn applied to its bind direction (the race driver's arms and legs), or the line between two bones (a menu figure's arm from shoulder to wrist, its leg from thigh to ankle: one rigid library arm cannot bend at the elbow). A skeleton with elbow, wrist and hand bones also twists the hands.
- **Joints** (`minifig-joints`): the figure moves only as a LEGO minifigure can. The torso and hips are one piece: the pelvis carries it by the hip axle, and it turns there by the torso bone's turn relative to the pelvis (a lean). Each leg swings on the hip axle (-110 to 60 degrees from hanging), each arm on its shoulder pin (all the way round), toward where the skeleton's limb points; whatever of that direction lies along the pin (a sideways reach no LEGO hinge makes) is dropped. The head turns only about the upright neck, by the twist of the head bone about it (up to 135 degrees; the original looks back over its shoulder at 111), never tilting off the shoulders. A hand stays on the end of its arm and twists on its pin by the hand bone's twist about the forearm (up to 90 degrees). Helmets wear their visor part (`2447`, trans-black on every helmet (trans-clear lit as an opaque white slab)), placed at the helmet's own origin. Before and after pictures: `~/ldraw-gallery/minifig-assembly/`.

## The look

Same plastic and the same studio as the cars. three.js lights every material by all lights of a scene, so a scene gets the studio once (`ensureStudioLights`, `createCarStudio`): the first car of the scene moves it with itself (a studio that a wheel or a driver brought first is taken over by it), a scene without a car (a menu) gets it, turned to light from the front for the standing figure and from behind for the race, the way each is looked at. The menus' figure carries its own studio, in its own frame (front is +x), so a screen that turns the figure still lights its face; its key is 1.3 times a car's.

## The gallery

`/ldraw-racer-gallery.html` on the dev server (not part of the build) exposes `renderWheels(chassisIndex, view, width, height, milliseconds)`, `renderWheelSheet(columns, cell, view, isGameBesideLDraw, first, count)` (the original's wheel beside the LDraw one for each chassis), `renderDriver(partBytes, 'race' | 'menu', view, width, height, clip, milliseconds)`, `renderDriverSheet`, `renderChampion(name, view, width, height)` and `renderCatalogueSheet(slot, indices, baseBytes, columns, cell)` (every hat, face, torso or leg of the catalogue on a seated driver) for a headless browser to call and save the canvas. The renders in `~/ldraw-gallery/drivers/` come from it.

`/ldraw-models.html` on the dev server (not part of the build) browses every `.ldr` and `.mpd` under `~/ldraw-models`, subfolders (`drafts/`, `chassis/`) included, from `/__development/ldraw-models/files.json` (the game itself still reads only `index.json`, so drafts never load into it): orbit and zoom by touch or mouse, Manual or Photo look, the original body or chassis beside a file named like a car code or chassis, the part count and header comments, and a reload when the file changes on disk. `#<path>` selects a file.

## Open

- The ghost cars and the champions' car bodies are the original's.
- Many rows of the review table are `guess` (faces and torsos of the non-champion drivers matched by look); hats the library cannot match (the cobra helm, the blue and gold nemes print) are approximations; five torsos keep the game's picture.
- The race driver's bind pose is a measured pose of the box body (`leg_box`), the LDraw legs sit forward as a minifigure sits; they do not follow the box's exact outline.
