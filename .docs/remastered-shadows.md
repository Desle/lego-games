# Real shadows (remastered layer)

With `?hd` in development, races get real shadows. Everything lives in `packages/lego-racers-web/src/modules/remastered/services/real-shadows/`. Read [remastered.md](remastered.md) first for the rule.

## Choices

`shadows=classic` keeps the original's blob decals (`carshadN`), `shadows=real` draws real shadows and hides the blobs, `shadows=scenery` (the default with `?hd`, the user's choice) also lets the static scenery (the BSP tree: walls, bridge posts, rails; and the plain mesh instances) cast, from a map baked once per track (below). The developer panel's **Shadows (reloads)** selector sets the parameter (Classic blobs / Real / Real + scenery). `sun=<azimuth>,<elevation>[,<ambient share>]` overrides the sun (degrees, in the track's own frame: azimuth from +x towards +y, elevation above the horizon); `sun=estimate` uses the estimator.

## How it works

- Neutral extension point `ViewerExtension.getRaceRenderPass(request)`: the race viewport asks each extension for a pass when it is made and calls `beforeRender()` before every frame; with no extension nothing changes. Two neutral tags go with it: the track's instance groups carry `userData.instanceKind`, and the blob decal meshes are named `RACER_SHADOW_DECAL_NAME`.
- Shadow map: one depth map (2048 x 2048, hardware comparison, `LessEqualCompare`) drawn from the sun with an orthographic camera 160 units wide, centred 25 units ahead of the camera and moved in whole texels along the light's axes (the map does not shimmer when the camera turns or moves). Casters are every solid mesh of the track scene except the track's static instances, decals, particles and blended meshes (shields, explosions, glass): cars (Classic or LDraw), wheels, drivers, power-up bricks, projectiles, and the track's skeleton instances (hazards).
- Baked scenery shadow (`shadows=scenery`): at the first frame the static scenery (every solid mesh of the BSP trees, all leaves whether or not the camera sees them, and the plain mesh instances at their nearest level of detail) is drawn once, depth only, from the sun with an orthographic camera fitted to the whole track's bounds in the light's axes (`baked-scenery-shadow.ts`). One map, longer side 4096 texels (2048 when the screen's short side is under 900 px, never beyond `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`), the other side in proportion so texels are square (Royal Knights: 0.7 units a texel at 2048, the forest track 0.48 at 4096). It costs about 67 MB at 4096 and 17 MB at 2048 (24-bit depth plus the 8-bit colour target) and is bought once: no per-frame scenery draw, and no radius, so far shadows are present and nothing pops. No tiling: every WebGL 2 device here allows 4096. The track's receivers take the darker of the live map and the baked map. Moving hazards (skeleton instances) are never baked.
- Live map: the 160-unit map around the camera now holds only the dynamic casters (cars, wheels, drivers, bricks, projectiles, hazards); it fades out radially from 35 to 95 percent of its half width instead of cutting off.
- Receivers: the track's own opaque materials. They stay `MeshBasicMaterial` (baked colour, no lights); `onBeforeCompile` multiplies the colour by a shade factor from the map. Nothing is lit twice. The shade factor is the baked light's own ratio: the baked brightness is `ambient + diffuse * cos`, so where the sun is blocked a surface that faces the sun by `cos` keeps `ambient / (ambient + diffuse * cos)` of it; surfaces turned from the sun are unchanged. PCF is 3 x 3 taps of the hardware-filtered comparison, normal-offset by two texels and depth-biased by 1.5 texels; the map fades out near its edge.
- LDraw receivers: the LDraw plastic (cars, wheels, drivers, LDraw pickups, and the game bricks drawn as plastic) is lit, not baked, so it receives through the `@lego-games/ldraw` material module's one lighting slot (`setPlasticLightingExtension`), filled by `plastic-shadow.ts` when the mode is not `classic` (with `classic` the slot stays empty and the plastic is unchanged). The slot scales only the direct lights (the studio's lamps: diffuse, specular, clear coat; in the stylised looks the shade and highlight), never the ambient, hemisphere or reflected studio. The GLSL that reads the maps (`getSunVisibility`, `shadow-sampling.ts`) is the one the track uses, and the uniforms are one page-wide set (`shadow-uniforms.ts`) that each race's pass fills and its `dispose` empties. Self-shadowing: the live map holds the receiving car itself, so the plastic reads it from a point lifted 6 units towards the sun (`PLASTIC_LIVE_SHADOW_LIFT`, more than a car's depth along the sun): a car never shades itself (no acne, no dark half against the studio's key light); another car or a hazard shades it only from more than that height above it. Leaving the car out of the map instead would need a map (or an id channel) per car, an extra pass. The baked map holds no car, so it is read two of its texels towards the sun, off the ground the wheels stand on. How dark: in the manual look (the default) a shaded car goes from about 1.0 to the look's floor of 0.72 of its colour; the photo look loses its whole key light.
- Cost: a depth-only draw of the casters (about 8 racers' meshes), one 4 MB depth target, and 9 taps per track fragment (18 with scenery); the LDraw plastic adds the same taps per plastic fragment, no pass and no target. Measured on the development Mac (headless Chrome, GPU, 640 x 480, Royal Knights frames 150 to 249 with all eight racers, Classic bricks): no difference above the noise (2.2 s for 100 rendered frames, including the simulation and the pixel read-back, with blobs, with real shadows and with scenery). Scenery mode adds one baked draw at load (8 to 10 ms of submission on the development Mac under SwiftShader) and one more shadow lookup per fragment.

## The sun: what the baked colours say (hypothesis)

The brief asked for the sun to be estimated from the prelit colours. `estimateSun` does that (`sun-estimation.ts`: weighted least squares of each triangle's brightness against `max(0, normal . direction)` inside each material, so a material's own colour does not count; a 6 degree grid over the hemisphere, then a fine search; the spec recovers a synthetic sun within 3 degrees) and was run on six tracks (`TRACK_SUN_ESTIMATES`): the best light explains only 2.5 to 11 percent of the variance (the track's materials, all prelit, show no consistent gradient along any direction: the colours are authored, not N . L). So **the estimate is not trusted** and is not the default: every track uses `DEFAULT_SUN` (azimuth -60, elevation 55 degrees, ambient share 0.45) unless `TRACK_SUNS` has it (four tracks: Royal Knights, the pirate bridge, the space station and the night forest with a faint high moon). Stored values are chosen by eye on renders so the shadows fall to a side the chase camera sees. A better source would be the sky's sun or moon position (not read yet), or the baked cast shadows of tall scenery.

## Open

- Sun directions per track are a choice, not recovered; the shadow direction can disagree with the sky.
- The warp tunnel and effect models cast nothing; shields and glass cast nothing; alpha-tested casters (none seen) would cast their whole quad.
- A track's baked shadows (if any) stay and are darkened again where a car's shadow falls on them.
