# Pixel trace: what the viewer puts on the screen, against the original

The golden, render and decal traces compare the **data**: simulation state, triangles in world or screen space. None of them looks at what the viewer's WebGL output contains, so a bug between the data and the pixels slips through. `pnpm run pixel-trace` closes that gap: it draws the original's frame in software, runs the **real viewer** in a headless Chrome, and compares the two images pixel by pixel.

`pnpm run pixel-trace` needs the original files, the emulator's virtualenv and a Chrome (`/Applications/Google Chrome.app`, `google-chrome`, or `CHROME_PATH`); without them the specs skip. It is not part of `pnpm run ci`. Each case takes 10 to 60 seconds.

## The two images

**Reference.** The emulator records every Direct3D draw of a frame (`render_trace.py`, see [render-trace.md](render-trace.md)): screen-space vertices, indices, the render and stage states, the texture surface. `rasteriseFrame` (`presentation-trace` module, `controller/pixel-trace/`) draws them into 640 x 480 RGBA: pixel centres at +0.5 and the top-left rule, UVs perspective correct with the recorded `1/w`, colours linear in screen space, the 16-bit surfaces decoded through their masks, point or bilinear sampling and wrap or clamp from the stage state, the depth test (less or equal) and write, texture operations select-diffuse, select-texture and modulate, the alpha test and the blend factors, strips and fans as well as lists. The texels equal to the surface's colour key are skipped when render state 41 is set (finding 7). The HUD (the draws after the last depth-tested one) is drawn too (finding 6). Not drawn: mip-mapping, specular. The clear colour is the viewer's, because the original's sky covers the frame.

**Port.** `pixel-trace-browser.ts` starts a Vite development server of its own (from port 5400 up, never the developer's) and a headless Chrome with its own profile and software WebGL (SwiftShader, so the pixels do not depend on the graphics card), and opens `/?pixel-trace`. In development `main.tsx` installs `window.runPixelTrace` instead of the game (`pixel-trace-harness.ts`). The runner loads the race from the development routes (`LEGO.JAM`, `LEGORacers.exe`) exactly as the trace scenario does, steps it with the scenario's frame times, controls and power-up grants (`runRaceFrames`, shared with the other traces), drives the real `createRaceViewport` (`update`, `showHazards`, `animate`, `render`) at 640 x 480, and copies the canvas into a 2D canvas in the same task to read the pixels. The camera is the port's own chase camera, not the original's matrix: a camera error shows as a difference.

The **HUD** is not part of the WebGL canvas: the viewer lays a 2D canvas (and the HUD's whole images as page elements) over it. The harness paints the frame's HUD commands on a canvas of the stage's size with the overlay's own painter (`services/hud-painter`) and composites it over the WebGL pixels before reading them.

What is **not** the viewer's own path: the race loop of `useRaceController` (menus, ghost cars, pause, audio) is replaced by the scripted loop; the viewport, the materials, the scene objects and the decals are the viewer's.

## The comparison

`comparePixels` marks a pixel when any colour channel differs by more than the tolerance (32 levels: the surfaces are 5 or 6 bits per channel and the port filters differently). It reports:

- the share of differing pixels per frame;
- the regions: 16-pixel cells with at least 12 differing pixels, joined into 8-connected regions, each with its bounding box, the mean colour of its differing pixels in the original and in the port, and the **original textures under them** (named by the port texture they match, `matchTextures`, or by hash when none matches), so a failure says what is missing or wrong;
- per original texture, the pixels it owns and how many differ.

A front-end case (`front-end-pixel-cases.ts`) is a list of keys, an optional pushed screen, the frames to record and the threshold (see Front-end cases). A race case (`pixel-trace-cases.ts`) is a `TraceScenarioRequest` of the render trace plus the largest share of differing pixels on its worst frame and, optionally, `watchedTextures`: a texture whose own pixels may differ at most so much (the oil slick's picture, so the slick's few pixels cannot hide in the frame's total). Set `PIXEL_TRACE_DUMP=<folder>` to write each frame's reference and port RGBA to files for analysis with numbers (`<case>-<frame>-reference.rgba` and `-port.rgba`, 640 x 480 x 4 bytes). Add `--silent=false` to vitest to print the report of a passing case.

## Adding a case

Add an entry to `PIXEL_TRACE_CASES`, run `pnpm run pixel-trace` with a threshold of 1 to read the baseline, set the threshold to the measurement plus a margin, and document anything open below. A change to what the viewer draws is done only when its case passes (`.docs/rules.md`).

## Finding 1: decal textures were sampled upside down

The first case, `Royal Knights, oil slick` (the slick is dropped at frame 305; frames 306 to 330 have it behind the car), failed on main: 2.56 percent of the frame differed at frame 306 and **35 percent of the pixels the original paints with the oil picture (`oilslk64`)** were wrong. Two regions, mirrored about the screen's centre line: the original's dark pool at x 219 to 301, y 417 to 479 (mean colour 15) where the port showed the plain road (84), and the port's pool in the other half (16 where the original has the road, 86). The decal's triangles and picture coordinates equal the original's (`decal-trace`), and so do the original's draws (`u`, `v` of the draw equal the decal's stored values). What differs is the **picture**: the original uploads the decal pictures `oilslk64` and `skid` exactly as stored (the surface equals the bitmap, top row first, checked with `matchTextures` against both row orders), while the viewer reversed the rows of every texture (finding 8 of [render-trace.md](render-trace.md): the textures the DLL loads for worlds and models hold the last row first). The pool's picture is not symmetric, so the blob landed on the wrong part of the decal's quad and the decal showed the road, as a few stray patches.

Fix: `createDecalMaterial` creates its texture with the rows kept (`createSceneMaterial(..., isRowOrderKept)`). Afterwards the picture's own pixels differ by less than 5 percent (the case asserts it) and frame 306 reads 0.64 percent.

**Hypothesis, not traced:** which texture-creation path leaves the rows as stored (the decals' pictures come from the power-up library `POWERUP.MDB` and the race's own materials through the exe, not through the DLL's world loader) has not been read from `LEGORacers.exe`; the evidence is the surface comparison above for the oil slick and the skid marks. The barrel scars and the crane's shadow use the same material code; their pixels stay inside their cases' thresholds, which is no proof of the row order.

## Finding 2: the introduction camera is a bone of the track's animation

Before this finding the introduction case differed in 51.5 percent of its pixels at frame 5: the whole scene was displaced. The port drew its chase camera behind the car while the original flies the camera along the track world's animation. `Camera_Update` (`0x00428540`) first checks the camera object's lock (`[camera object +0x28]`, set per local player by `0x00434340` from race `+0x3ac`, released by `Race_UpdateState1` `0x004349a0` with `0x004047a0`); a locked camera runs only `0x004047b0` and returns, leaving the chase state untouched (no field-of-view effect, no sound listener). The lock is `(skeleton instance, bone)` of the camera object that `0x00432e20` looks up by the RAB name (keyword `0x49`, `camDUM`) in the track world's camera table (`0x1002dc80`: file keyword `0x2f` holds the skeleton instance index and then the bone index; `camDUM` is instance 1, bone 1).

`0x004047b0` reads the bone's translation (bone slot `+0x40`) and rows 0 and 2 of its rotation (`+0x20`), walks the parents from the bone's parent up (`+0x4` for the point, `+0xc` for the two vectors), then carries the point through the instance's frame scaled (`+0x2c`) and the vectors by its rotation (`+0x34`), and hands the camera node the position (`+0x44`) and the orientation `(row 0, -row 2)` (`+0x24`). Ported as `getSkeletonInstanceCameraView` (`world-animation`); `getIntroductionCameraView` (`race-presentation`) uses it while `RaceState.introductionCameraObject` is set, and the chase camera state does not advance in that time. The result: the case reads 1.26 percent at frame 5 (threshold 1.5 percent). Of that, 0.49 percent does not disappear when each pixel may match a neighbour two pixels away (a colour difference); the rest is silhouette edges.

**Hypothesis, not traced:** the camera node's orientation slot `+0x24` takes `(forward, up)` in that order (the evidence is the pixels). The release effect of `0x004349a0` (`0x00428390`, `0x004282a0` seed the chase camera from the racer) is not ported: the racers have not moved since the load, so the frozen chase state stands in. A mirrored race is not handled by the lock (the position follows the mirrored instance, the vectors are not mirrored).

**Open, the plain race frame (3.2 percent at frames 100 and 170, 2.2 percent later):** (1) the skid marks (`skid`: 20 to 40 percent of its pixels; the original draws them at 15 to 37, the port at 80 to 95; same cause as the power slide case, the diffuse colour hypothesis above); (2) the two textures the matcher names by hash, `c8a290b0cd5475c2` and `3dabd45342464cf7`, in the upper part of the picture (x 129 to 374, y 5 to 141): the port is about twice as bright (64,65,116 against 33,41,77), the same region differs in the introduction frame, so it is not the camera or the dark-zone colour shift; (3) the unnamed `cfdfc3bfd771fdf7` and `blank` pictures on the road ahead (the port is brighter, 144,134,125 against 95,88,86); (4) a band of `rkfence` at y 194 to 239 (the port is darker and bluer, 56,76,91 against 90,91,94). None of these is the camera.

## Finding 3: the warp's blue fade was not drawn (the screen fader)

Before the three findings of this and the next two sections the warp case differed 23.5, 99.6, 30.8, 45.1, 52.0 and 4.7 percent on frames 310, 320, 330, 345, 360 and 375 (its threshold was a placeholder of 100 percent); it now reads 0.7, 0.0, 0.2, 0.3, 1.2 and 4.7, with a threshold of 5 percent and the tunnel (`titwat2`, 2 percent) and the portal (5 percent of the pictures' own pixels) watched.

The warp case differed 99.6 percent at frame 320: the original's frame there is one flat colour (0, 0, 99), the tunnel is under a full-screen quad. The quad is the race's **screen fader** (race `+0x27fc`, two entries of 0x18 bytes, `0x00494e10`): `0x0045d780` (the warp's update, vtable slot `+8`) starts an entry of 200 ms, colour (0, 0, 0x64), fading in, when a human's warp is in its starting state with less than 200 ms to go (`0x0045d780` compares the timer before the frame's countdown); `Race_Update` (`0x00435220`) runs the fader's update `0x00494f60` with the frame's step after the power-ups; `0x004905e0` draws every entry in use as a quad over the owner's view with alpha `ftol(255 * (1 - left / total))` (`*(0x004b09c0)` = 255.0; flag bit 1 reverses it for a fade-out). An entry whose clock ran out lives one more update and then resets (`0x00490590`). Alpha per frame at 33 ms: 42, 84, 126, 168, 210, 252, then 255, 255 (original frames 315 to 322, matched). While the player is carried the warp's own draw (`0x0045d940` calls `0x00494fa0` with the fader) and `Race_Render` (`0x004357e0`) both draw it, so it lies over the view twice.

Port: `race/types/screen-fader` (the entries; a differential spec for `0x00490550` and `0x00490590`), `race/controller/warp-fade` (the start, in `updateRace`), `race-presentation` (`screenFades`), `screen-fade-overlay` (the viewer's quads after the scene). Not wired yet, and the fader makes it easy: the race start's fade-in (`0x00433525`, 500 ms) and the finish fades (`0x00434ee8`, `0x004351ab`), the other callers of `0x00494eb0`. The alpha of `0x004905e0` has no differential spec (it draws through the device); the pixel case is its check.

## Finding 4: the warp changes the field of view (and the viewer ignored the camera's)

Frames 330 to 360 showed the tunnel at the wrong size. `0x0045d940` sets the view's field of view (the camera object's `+0x148` frame, `+8`) to `table[(-ftol(timer * pi / 1500 * -162.97466) - 0x100) & 0x3ff] * 45 + base` for the draw and puts the base back afterwards; `base` is power-up system `+0x54`, which `Race_LoadTrackContent` (`0x00433480`) fills from game settings `+0xc` (less 25 with two local players). **Hypothesis:** `+0xc` is 65, the camera's initial field of view: with 65 the tunnel frames match (0.2 to 1.2 percent of the frame). The starting state also eases the view: `0x0045d560` sets the camera's base (`+0x134`) to `base + 30` and `0x0045dc90` (state 2 to 3) puts the view and the base back, so frame 310 (the portal) needs the camera's own view effect (`0x00427e80`, ported as `stepCameraFovEffect` for the curse) to reach the viewer, which until now read a constant 65: `RacePresentationFrame.fieldOfViewDegrees` and `WarpView.fieldOfViewDegrees`. This also applies the curse's field-of-view shake to the viewer (not covered by a pixel case yet).

## Finding 5: alpha blending brightened the picture (the canvas's alpha)

With the field of view right, the portal (a 75 percent blend) still showed 25 percent too bright: the effective blend weights were 0.92 and 0.28, which add to 1.2 = 1 / 0.81, and 0.81 is the alpha the blend `(source alpha, 1 - source alpha)` leaves in the canvas's own alpha channel (0.75 * 0.75 + 0.25). The canvas is composited, and read back, with its alpha un-premultiplied. Direct3D's surface has no alpha, so the blends of the viewer now keep the surface alpha (`blendSrcAlpha` zero, `blendDstAlpha` one, `KEPT_SURFACE_ALPHA`): every blended material was affected (frame 310: 6.2 to 0.7 percent). With the portal's vertex alpha lowered to 0.5 as an experiment the frame also improved (to 2.7 percent), which is how the double effect first showed.

## Finding 6: the race start's fade-in was not drawn, and the HUD joined the reference

The reference used to leave out the draws after the last depth-tested one (the HUD). It now includes them (`isHudIncluded`), and the port image has the HUD composited over the WebGL pixels: the harness paints the HUD commands of the frame (`presentation.hudFrame`) on a 2D canvas the size of the stage with the overlay's own painter (`services/hud-painter`, the code `RaceHudOverlay` runs in an effect; the overlay's whole-image `<img>` elements are painted on the canvas with smoothing, as the page would show them) and lays it over the viewport's pixels. The first run with the HUD reported the introduction frame at **59.3 percent**: the original draws a full-screen quad there. It is the race's screen fader again: `Race_LoadTrackContent` (`0x00433480`) starts the fader's first entry at `0x00433525` with `0x00494eb0(500, 1, 0, 0)`: 500 ms, the fade-out flag set, no camera, the reset black of `0x00490530`. Ported in `createRace` (`startScreenFade`, with a spec); the introduction frame reads **0.12 percent**. The finish fades (`0x00434ee8`, `0x004351ab`) are wired in finding 9.

## Finding 7: the colour key (render state 41) is part of the reference, and the decals use it

The reference now honours the colour key. The emulator records each surface's source colour key (`ddckCKSrcBlt` of the surface description and `SetColorKey`, `com_display.py`; the texture line has `colorKey`), the rasteriser skips the texels equal to it when render state 41 is set (the nearest texel stands in for a filtered sample). Menu fonts and images all carry the key `(0, 0)`: with it the front-end cases dropped from 15 to 8 percent (main menu) and the race HUD's minimap and digits stopped showing black boxes. The pictures of the scene also carry keys (the fence `25388`, ...), so the netting case fell from 6.5 to 1.5 percent.

**Open, found by it:** the decals' draws have render state 41 set with the key `(0, 0)` and blending on (`oilslk64`, `skid` and the third picture, states 5). `createDecalMaterial` keeps the colour key off ("the device's alpha override", `dynamic-mesh.ts`), so the viewer draws the black texels of the picture, which the original discards. At frame 306 of the oil slick case the port shows pure black patches (0, 0, 0) beside the dark pool where the original shows the road (56): 5,400 pixels, 2.25 percent of the frame (the case read 0.64 before, because the reference drew the black too). The same cause is a candidate for the skid marks that every case lists as open ("darker in the original" in finding 1 may be the other way round: the viewer draws black, the original discards it). Fixed in finding 9.

## Finding 8: the buttons' text colours were the wrong ones

The main menu case showed every button label grey (59, 59, 66) where the original draws dark yellow (97, 88, 14) and the focused one bright yellow (181, 169, 6) where the port drew green. A button style has six text colours (grey, grey, dark yellow, green, yellow, green); the port took the first for a normal button and the sixth for the focused one (hypothesis in `front-end.md`). The pixels give normal = the third, focused = the fifth, disabled = grey (`BUTTON_COLOR_INDEX`, `widget-commands.ts`). The images and fonts of a button keep the old index (not checked). The "vs" button is grey in the original because the emulator has no gamepad (`controllerCount` 0, which is also what the viewer's input source reports without one), so the front-end harness uses 0.

## Finding 9: the decals keep the colour key, and the finish fades

**The key of the decals.** `createDecalMaterial` switched the alpha test off on the reasoning that the decals draw under an alpha of their own (the device's override, `decal.md`). That override is true of the recorded state 15 (alpha test off), but state 41 is on for the decals and their pictures carry a key, so the original drops the keyed texels. The port's colour key is the alpha test on the picture's alpha (the bitmap loader `0x100200f0` gives the key colour alpha 0; the device path `0x100` of `0x100257e0`); the decal material now keeps the material's own. No shader change was needed: the keyed alpha is the texture's, the decal's vertex alpha multiplies it, and a faded decal (alpha 0) is not drawn either way. `oilslk64` (blend off, key 0,0,0) lost its opaque black rectangle: the oil slick case reads **0.76 percent** at frame 306 (2.25 before), the worst frame is now the HUD digits and the hash-named road pictures.

The skid marks every case listed as "darker in the original" were never the skid trails: the port has no active trail in these cases (the trails exist only while the tire smoke runs). The textures the matcher names `skid` are the **racers' shadow pictures** (`decal.md`, "Not decals": the shadow pictures and the sky match `skid` best; vertex alpha `0xbf`, `0xb9`, `0xa3` fading with the distance, the same alpha the port gives, checked at frames 100, 170 and 306). The reference used to draw the black background of those pictures (the key), which is what the earlier baseline measured as "15 to 37 against 80 to 95".

**Hypothesis, open (the shadow pictures):** the original's shadow surfaces are 64 x 64 of two colours (0, the key, and 8) in four to five different shapes (2,128 / 1,888 / 1,915 / 1,916 texels of 8), while the port's `carshad0..5` bitmaps hold one blocky shape of 16 with 1,440 keyed texels. The shadows remain among the textures with differing pixels in every case (hundreds to a thousand pixels a frame).

**The finish fades.** `Race_UpdateState4` (`0x00434eb0`) starts a 1,000 ms fade to black at `0x00434ee8` when its state timer (race `+0x332c`) passes 9,000 ms, no fade is running (`0x00494f30`) and the race has no points record (race `+0x18` is zero); `Race_UpdateState5` (`0x00435180`) does the same at `0x004351ab` from 4,000 ms with no further condition. Both call `0x00494eb0(1000, 0, 0, 0)`: the fade-out flag clear (the colour fades in), black, no camera (the whole surface). Ported in `race/controller/phase-fade`, called first thing in `stepFinishing` and `stepResults` (a race that reaches results from state 4 sets the timer to 0 before calling state 5, so it starts no second fade there). The pixel cases end before the finish, so a spec of `phase-fade` checks the thresholds and the one-fade rule; the quad itself is the fader's, already compared by the warp and introduction cases.

## Finding 10: the time trial (ghost cars)

`isTimeTrial` in a scenario sets game mode 2 (`race_session.py`, settings `+0x24`), which makes `RaceSession_Begin` run `Ghost_Initialize` and load the champion's run, and the harness steps the ghost playback with the race (`runRaceFrames` calls `getGhostCars` after each step, as `0x00435a00` runs `0x00422960` after the race's update, so the rate the playback stores reaches the next sub-step). The first run of `RACEC0R0, time trial ghost` differed in **34 percent** of the frame at every frame: the original puts the player on grid slot 2 in a time trial (`0x0043ba2c`, found by reading the player's position in the emulator: (379.5, 210.5) against slot 0's (368.3, 194.0)), the port on slot 0. After the slot, 2.2 to 3.7 percent remain: the HUD of a time trial (the timers at the top right are grey in the original and blue in the viewer, an item at the top left), the countdown lights of frames 140 to 168 (yellow in the original, cyan in the viewer: the red and blue channels swapped, matched to `skid` by mistake) and the plain race's textures, **open** and not examined. The ghost itself: with the device's alpha override (`0x7f`, found as vertex alpha `0x7f` and blending in the recorded draws) its pictures differ in 1.5 percent of their pixels, 47.7 percent without; with its wheel and driver clips 1.5 percent for the driver's picture, 11.5 percent without. The case watches the two pictures of `GHOST.WDB` (`2d13aec07aff6e6c`, `867676f1b4ecf538`).

## Front-end cases

`front-end-pixel-trace.trace.spec.ts` (also run by `pnpm run pixel-trace`) runs the real front end. **Reference:** `front_end_trace.py` with the scenario's keys (taps) and, for screens the keys cannot reach, `pushScreens` (frame 200); the draws of the recorded frames go through the same `rasteriseFrame` (all draws, black clear). **Port:** `/?pixel-trace` also installs `runFrontEndPixelTrace` (`front-end-pixel-harness`, a controller): it fetches LEGO.JAM and the executable from the development routes, creates the front end (`createFrontEnd`, optionally on a pushed screen), steps it with the scripted inputs and 33 ms, draws **every** step with the viewer's own `createFrontEndRenderer` (the 2D canvas, the bitmap fonts and the menus' 3D items in `menu-viewport`; `draw` takes the scripted frame time for the 3D animation instead of the clock) and reads the pixels of the recorded steps. The mapping of the frames is the one of the cinema traces: the original opens its main menu in frame 154 (a pushed screen shows in frame 200); port step n shows original frame start + n, and a key tapped in frame f is the input of the step that produces frame f + 1. The textures of the port that the matcher names are the front end's images and fonts (named by file); the pictures of the 3D items stay hashes.

Reference limits: the emulated mouse sits at (0, 0), where the original draws a 32 x 32 yellow picture (`25426fefa874aad5`, 661 pixels in every screen; **hypothesis:** the cursor), which the viewer does not draw; the filtered colour-key edge is approximated by the nearest texel.
